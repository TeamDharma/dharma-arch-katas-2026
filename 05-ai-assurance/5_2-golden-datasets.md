# Golden datasets

*How we build evaluation data for a system that has never run.*

## The problem nobody else in this kata has to solve twice

Greenfield means the estate has never instrumented anything. On opening day
there is no attendance history, no machine-readable feeding records, no labeled
images of animals, and no transcript of a single visitor question.

Every evaluation approach in the literature assumes a dataset. We do not have
one, and we cannot wait three years to get one, because the Countess needs
revenue on the new system by month six.

So the datasets have to be manufactured, and the honest thing is to be explicit
about how, and about what each method is worth.

## Four sources, in descending order of trustworthiness

### 1. Domain expert construction

A keeper writes down the twenty things that indicate an animal is unwell, and
what each looks like per species. A veterinarian reviews it. That is a golden
set, built by the people whose judgment the system is meant to support.

Slow, expensive, and the most trustworthy thing available before launch. Used
for: animal welfare thresholds, safety-classified concierge topics, notifiable
disease indicators.

### 2. Transfer from comparable operations

Attraction attendance patterns, zoo husbandry records, and ride maintenance
intervals are not secret. Published data from comparable operations gives a
starting distribution for demand forecasting and maintenance intervals.

Useful for shape, misleading for magnitude. A Bavarian estate is not a Florida
theme park. We use transferred data to initialize and local data to correct,
and we say which is which.

### 3. Synthetic generation with human review

For the concierge, we generate candidate visitor questions across intents,
languages, and phrasings, then have a human keep, fix, or discard each one. The
generation is cheap, the review is what makes it a golden set.

The failure mode is well known and we guard against it: synthetic questions
generated by a model tend to be the questions that model answers well. So the
review step specifically hunts for questions a real visitor would ask and the
generator did not produce. Real transcripts replace synthetic entries as soon as
they exist.

### 4. Production traffic, once it exists

From month four onward, real inputs become the primary source. Sampled,
labeled, and folded back in. Every adversarial attempt observed goes straight
into the adversarial suite.

This is the only source that actually represents the input distribution, which
is why the others are scaffolding rather than the building.

## Dataset per capability

| Capability | Initial source | Size at launch | Replaced by production data from, counted from that capability's launch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Voice concierge, correctness | Synthetic plus expert review | 300 questions | Month 4 |
| Voice concierge, adversarial | Published attack corpora plus our own | 200 attempts | Continuously, on observation |
| Voice concierge, safety topics | Expert written, veterinarian reviewed | 60 questions | Never fully. Expert review remains authoritative |
| Animal welfare, aquatic | Keeper and vet constructed, per species | 55 enclosure profiles | Month 9, as incidents accumulate |
| Animal welfare, terrestrial | Keeper and vet constructed, per species | Per species profiles | Month 9 |
| Piranha population | Physical counts, calibration set | 12 counts over 6 months | Ongoing calibration, never replaced |
| Demand forecasting | Transferred comparable data plus weather and holiday calendars | 2 seasons synthetic | Month 12, after one real season |
| Predictive maintenance | Manufacturer intervals plus inspection history | Per ride | Month 18 |
| Accessibility and translation | Expert reviewed reference translations | 150 passages per language | Month 6 |

## The rule that keeps this honest

**A capability may not ship above its heuristic baseline until its golden set is
built and it beats the baseline by the defined margin.** Not "until the model
looks good in a demo". The dataset gates the deployment, which means someone has
to do the unglamorous work of building it before the interesting work counts.

This is the practical consequence of FF-20, and it is the main reason our
phasing puts AI capabilities in phase 2 rather than phase 1. The data has to
exist first.

## What we are honest about

A 300 question golden set for a concierge serving 15,000 visitors a day is
small. It will not represent the tail. It is what four people can build and
review properly before launch, and a small well-reviewed set beats a large
carelessly generated one, because the large one gives false confidence at
exactly the moment confidence matters most.

The size is a starting position, not a target. The real target is the month
four replacement with production traffic, and the gap between launch and month
four is covered by a higher sampling rate and a conservative fallback, not by
pretending the dataset is adequate.
